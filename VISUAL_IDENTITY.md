# Visual identity

## Direction

This site is a personal home for Nicholas's projects and interests. It should feel curious, grounded, and welcoming, with a little of the character of an independent field journal. Birding, photography, music, and making things can inform the mood without turning the interface into a literal illustration of any one interest.

Keep forest green as the anchor, with fresh leaf greens, warm clay, and a touch of water blue on a light paper canvas. Project screenshots and photographs bring their own colors; give them room to stand on their own.

## Color and surfaces

The shared colors live in `src/styles/global.css`. Forest and deep forest ground headings, navigation, and primary actions. Leaf green gives panels and small decorative marks a lively lift; clay highlights details, and water blue identifies links and focus. The canvas is light, warm paper. Use accents with purpose rather than assigning a different color to every element.

Keep text and controls readable against their surfaces. Check contrast in normal, hover, active, and keyboard focus states. Do not rely on color alone to indicate the current page or an interaction state. Consolidate repeated color values into shared tokens as the palette is refined.

## Typography

The current pairing is Chicle for display headings and the site name, with Alegreya for body copy and controls. The display face gives the site its personality; use it with restraint so project names remain readable and do not overwhelm their descriptions. Keep body text and functional labels comfortably legible, with a consistent scale and modest differences between levels. Avoid tiny uppercase labels and excessive tracking.

## Layout and imagery

Projects is the entry point. Bring the project index and first project into view without large empty gaps, and maintain a clear relationship between each title, tagline, screenshot, description, and links. The About page should be brief and easy to scan. Use spacing to group related content, not as decoration for its own sake.

Let project screenshots show the actual work. Preserve their proportions and avoid recoloring or decorating the images. Rounded shapes, botanical marks, and a small offset shadow give the page a playful field journal character while keeping the interface quieter than the projects it presents.

## Navigation and interaction

Make Projects and About easy to find, with a clear current-page state. Use recognizable social icons alongside text labels. Keep links and controls large enough to use comfortably on touch screens, and provide visible keyboard focus. Motion should be subtle, quick, and respectful of reduced-motion preferences.

Use Phosphor icons for interface and decorative icons, with consistent sizing and a weight suited to their context. Decorative icons are hidden from assistive technology; icon-only controls need accessible names.

## Voice

Write in a direct, personable voice. Describe what a project does in plain language, credit its inspirations, and let its personality come through without marketing filler.

## Review

For visual changes, inspect Projects and About at desktop and mobile widths, including navigation, project links, social links, hover, and keyboard focus. Check that long project titles wrap cleanly and that images remain readable. Update this document when a visual decision becomes established.
